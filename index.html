Research Program

Christchurch Earthquakes and Residential Reallocation

with Ilan Noy

Using linked New Zealand administrative data, this research program studies how the Christchurch earthquakes reshaped residential mobility and population reallocation. The project examines migration out of and into affected areas, the persistence of relocation, and where people move after leaving Christchurch. It is designed as a broader research program rather than a single-paper project.

Working Papers

Implementation Capacity under Disaster Stress: Emergency Administrative Behavior and Fiscal Resilience

This paper studies whether visible emergency administrative behavior is a reliable signal of underlying government capacity and fiscal resilience. I develop a sequential model in which implementation capacity is accumulated before a disaster, while administrative shortcuts and recovery liabilities are determined afterward, and use precipitation disasters across Chinese prefectures as external administrative stress tests.

Better Credit Review, Less Aggregate Information: Endogenous Review Allocation

This theory paper studies endogenous information acquisition when scarce review capacity is allocated across heterogeneous credit exposures. Better review technology can improve inference under every fixed allocation while inducing a reallocation of review toward less systematically informative exposures, reducing information about the common credit condition.

Regulating Service Quality Under Selective Observability

This project studies regulation when public performance measures observe only a selected subset of economically relevant outcomes. Using New York City ride-hailing data, it examines how completed-service performance can improve while performance across submitted requests deteriorates, and develops a framework for deciding when regulators should escalate from routine public monitoring to richer information review.
